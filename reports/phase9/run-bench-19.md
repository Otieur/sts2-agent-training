# Comparison: elites fought while healthy, and healing before a boss, at Ascension 0 (run benchmark 19, two factors)

Written before the run (decision 0005, ML-10), 2026-11-22 at 04:45, after the results of run benchmarks 2–9, 11, 12 and 15 and before any result of run benchmarks 16 and 17. Of run benchmark 18, under way at the time, three things were known: the provisional stack reading noted in its section 6 (01:56: `rfm` in the stack on the seeds finished by then), the floors of two of its seeds seen in the queue's log (its section 6, 04:25), and the number of seeds recorded. Sections 1–4 are not edited after the run starts; corrections go in section 6 with the reason.

## 1. Hypotheses
- **H1 (elites, floors):** at Ascension 0, fighting the elites of acts 1 and 2 while healthy (`el`: entering one at 60% HP or more, keeping one on the route at 70% or more) reaches more floors than the stack's route choice alone.
- **H2 (rest before a boss, floors):** healing at the rest site before a boss below 80% HP (`br`) reaches more floors than healing there only below 50%.
- **H3, H4 (wins):** each of the two wins more runs.

Why elites: run benchmark 15 found that the Agent reaches each boss with fewer relics than the Player (3.4 against 5.1 at the act 1 boss, 5.9 against 9.9 at the act 2 boss) and with more HP at the act 1 boss (77% against 60%), after fewer elites (0.76 against 1.00 in act 1, 0.53 against 1.08 in act 2); its route rollouts took an elite at 32 of 194 forks that offered one. In the Agent's own runs at Ascension 0 (run benchmarks 11, 12 and 15) an elite is close to free while it is healthy: act 1 elites were won in 210 of 213 fights for 9 HP on average (179 of 179 when entered at 60% HP or more), act 2 elites in 62 of 84 (37 of 39 at 60% or more, 25 of 45 below), act 3 elites in 9 of 13. An elite gives a relic. In run benchmark 15 the routes met a fork with an elite as a next point 64 times in act 1 with HP at 60% or more and took it 22 times, and 33 times in act 2, taking it 4 times. Bosses beaten by relics held at the fight's start: act 1, 65% with fewer than three, 72% with three, 81% with four; act 2, 21% with four, 33% with five to seven, 57% with eight to ten.

Why the rest: the same fights by HP at the boss fight's start. Act 1 boss: won 94 of 101 at 80% HP or more, 83 of 123 at 60–80%, 18 of 40 at 40–60%. Act 2 boss: 8 of 14, 25 of 59, 12 of 58. The stack's rest rule (`smith`) upgrades a card at the last rest site unless HP is below 50%, and a rest heals 30% of the maximum. By upgraded cards the act 1 boss falls in 45% of fights with fewer than two, 76% with two or three, 83% with four or five; the act 2 boss between 31% and 40% whatever the count from four upward.

These are associations inside the Agent's own runs: a stronger deck keeps more HP, wins more elites and beats the boss more often, so none of the figures above is the effect of an elite or of a rest. That is what the run measures.

## 2. Runs
- 30 new seeds (`RB019` + index 00000–00029), Ironclad, **Ascension 0**, whole new runs in the simulator (`tools/run_bench.py`). Four policies play every seed.
- `G`: the stack after run benchmark 16 by that registration's rule, computed by the queue script (`data/learn/queue/stack3.py elbr`) before any result of this run.
- Arms: `G`, `G+el`, `G+br`, `G+el+br`.
- `el` (`tools/run_bench.py elite_moves`), in acts 1 and 2: at a map move where a next point is an elite and HP is at 60% of the maximum or more, only the elite points are considered. Otherwise, with HP at 70% or more, next points from which no elite of this act can be reached any more are left out when another next point still reaches one (the act's map is visible to the player). The stack's route choice decides among the points left, and alone in every other case and in act 3 (elites there were won in 9 of 13 fights for 21 HP). Both bars are asked again at every fork, so after an elite that cost HP the rule steps back. A rule component: it also acts inside every rollout, so cards, events and shops are judged along routes that meet elites.
- `br`: with `smith`, the rest site right before a boss heals below 80% HP instead of below 50%; other rest sites as before (heal below 35%). A rule component, also inside rollouts.
- Combat in all arms: `mcw6` in every fight, `--fight-rebuild save+run`. Host: the Release3 build; the fight-outcome model, if `rfm` is in `G`, is `fight-v1c`. 9 workers claim seeds in index order (`--dynamic`); a worker's evaluators share one evaluation host (run benchmark 16, section 6). Stopping rule: unfinished runs stop 6 hours after the start; time the machine is asleep or off does not count. The analysis uses the seeds for which all four policies finished.
- Fights and rollout decisions are recorded as in run benchmark 18.

## 3. Policies
- `G`: as fixed above.
- `G+el`: the same with elites fought while healthy.
- `G+br`: the same with the heal before a boss below 80% HP.
- `G+el+br`: both.

## 4. Metrics and tests
- A factor's effect on a seed: the mean floor of the two arms with it minus the mean floor of the two arms without it. H1 and H2: one-sided sign test at 0.05 across seeds, with the mean effect and a bootstrap 95% interval. H3 and H4: per seed, wins with the factor minus wins without (−2 to 2), one-sided exact sign test at 0.05 on the seeds where it is not zero; wins per arm with Wilson intervals (`tools/run_details.py --factor`). The interaction (the elite effect with `br` minus without) is reported, not tested: elites cost HP and the rest gives it back, so the two may need each other. The two factors are separate questions; no correction is applied across them.
- Checks that the treatments were applied: elites fought per act and per arm; forks where `el` acted (`el_entered` of `el_doors`, `el_narrowed` of `el_forks`); rests before a boss turned into heals by `br` (`br_heals`); HP, relics and upgraded cards at each boss fight's start; the rollout settings recorded with every decision.
- Secondary: elite fights won and HP lost in them, by act and by HP at the door; runs ended by an elite; act bosses beaten; where runs die; seconds per run.
- What follows, the stack that runs next: a factor's treatment enters the stack if its mean effect in floors is positive and its two arms together do not win fewer runs than the two arms without it (`stack3.py after-elbr`). A claim that a component helps needs its test.

## 6. Deviations and notes
- 2026-11-22 04:45, before the start, how the `el` rule got its form. Three versions were tried on seeds outside every benchmark (`RB096`, `RB097`; search in every fight; base `rcard+rpath+pot+smith+rshop2+ropt+rfm`, the provisional stack), at low priority beside run benchmark 18. Version 1 (HP at 60% or more: only the next points from which the most elites of the act can still be reached): on one seed three act 1 elites, no upgraded card and about 25% HP at the act 1 boss, lost there; the rule was judged too greedy on that one run, which is an outcome of a smoke seed and is said here for that reason. Version 2 (HP at 70% or more: next points from which no elite can be reached any more are left out): over two seeds one or two forks narrowed and hardly an elite added, because an elite stays reachable for most of an act while HP at its door is often below 70%, and the rule steps back there. Version 3, the one registered: the door rule at 60% and the reach rule at 70%, acts 1 and 2. The two bars come from the forks and elite fights of run benchmark 15 (section 1), not from the smoke seeds; what was looked at on those seeds was behaviour (elites fought per act, the rule's counters, HP, relics and upgraded cards at the bosses, errors), with the runs' floors on screen. Two or three seeds per version tell nothing about floors or wins.
- The last smoke test (three seeds `RB097`, four arms, version 3): act 1 elites per run 0.5 in the two arms without `el` (3 in 6 runs) and 1.33 in the two with it (8 in 6 runs); the door rule entered an elite at 4 of 4 doors; the reach rule met 18 forks and narrowed none (its unit tests cover the narrowing); `br` turned one rest before a boss into a heal; no search or rollout errors; every rollout decision records its own arm's rule policy (`path+pot+smith`, `el+path+pot+smith`, `br+path+pot+smith`, `br+el+path+pot+smith`). One run of an `el` arm died in an act 1 elite on floor 7, entered with 74% HP at a point with no other way on: the figures of section 1 come from elites the rollouts chose themselves, and with `el` inside the rollouts a route can meet an elite earlier, with a weaker deck. No bar was added for that; it is part of what the run measures.
- Unit tests of the rule (`tools/run_bench.py elite_moves`, on a made-up map): below 60% HP, in act 3 and at a single next point the rule returns nothing; an elite among the next points is entered at 60% or more and counted; when every next point is an elite nothing is narrowed; at 70% or more a next point that reaches no elite is left out when another reaches one. Of the rest rule: nine cases around the 35%, 50% and 80% bars, with and without `br`. A policy without `el` and `br` plays as before the change (checked by exact equality with the committed script on seeds `RB098`, section 6 of run benchmark 16).
- The run starts from the queue (`data/learn/queue/queue12.sh`) after run benchmark 16, only once this file is committed. The main process ends workers that are still running 10 minutes after the deadline (section 6 of run benchmark 16, 04:45).
- 2026-11-22 05:30, before the start: two changes to `tools/run_bench.py` that this run is played with. Both come out of run benchmark 18, whose results were read at 04:48 (`F` stays, the model's rollouts are not adopted, so `G` has no `rfm`); nothing of run benchmark 16 is known. (1) **A decision's sampled futures are numbered inside the run** (`evaluate`), no longer by a count a worker's evaluators kept over the worker's whole life, so a run does not depend on what its worker played before it (run benchmark 18, section 6, 04:50). Checked on seeds outside every benchmark: a policy played alone and after another policy in the same worker gives identical records apart from timings, with planner fights (`RB092`, two seeds; with the old numbering both runs differ from their first decision) and with search in every fight (`RB089`, one seed), and the first run a worker plays is identical under the old and the new code (`RB090`, `RB091`). A side effect for this run: decisions at the same place in two arms' runs now sample the same futures, where each arm's count used to run on from the last one's. (2) **A fight past turn 100 is given up** (`FIGHT_TURN_CAP`): the run ends there, is not won and counts with the floor it stands on; its record carries `stalled_fight`, and the reports list it apart ("given up in a fight that did not end"). Reason: in run benchmark 18 a deck left without an attack blocked an enemy for 566 turns, which cost a worker three hours and the comparison a seed; a player in that fight can only abandon the run, and a run that is cut off unrecorded hides the failure of the arm that built the deck. The cap is far from any real fight: of 880 fights in that run's log the longest took 56 decisions, and the fight without end took four decisions a turn. Unit test (`RB088`): with the cap set to 2 a run is given up in its first fight that passes turn 2 and recorded as such, the fights before it being those of the normal run. Section 4's secondary metrics gain one line: runs given up in a fight that did not end, per arm.
