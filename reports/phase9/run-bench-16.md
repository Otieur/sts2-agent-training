# Comparison: map routes by rollouts against the route planner, and a shop rule that buys, at Ascension 0 (run benchmark 16, two factors)

Written before the run (decision 0005 item 3, ML-10), 2026-11-21 at 14:48, after the results of run benchmarks 2–9 and 11 and before any result of run benchmarks 12 and 15; amended before the start to two factors (section 6). Sections 1–4 are not edited after the run starts; corrections go in section 6 with the reason.

## 1. Hypotheses
- **H1 (routes, floors):** on the stack that uses potions, upgrades and shops, choosing map moves by rollouts (`rpath`) still reaches more floors than the route planner (`path`).
- **H2 (shop rule, floors):** a shop rule that buys (`bshop`), played inside every rollout and wherever rollouts leave a shop to the rule, reaches more floors than the rule that leaves every shop.
- **H3, H4 (wins):** each of the two wins more runs.

Why routes: run benchmark 4 found +1.1 floors for route rollouts over the planner at A5 (p = 0.04, interval including zero), gained by avoiding elites, on a stack that drank no potion and upgraded Strikes. The rollouts judge a route with the rule planner fighting, which loses elite fights that search wins, so they keep away from elites: 1.3 elites per run in run benchmark 11, 9 elite choices in 282 forks. Elites are where relics come from, and the agent holds 3.4 relics at the act 1 boss against the Player's 5.3. With potions now used in elite fights the balance may have moved. The planner weighs expected loot against fatal risk from the Player's history and takes about two elites per run. The result says whether route rollouts keep their place until the fight-outcome model replaces the planner inside them.

Why the shop rule: every rollout (for cards, routes, shops and events) is played by the stack's rules, and the rule leaves every shop. So in a rollout gold buys nothing: a route through a shop, a card reward skipped for gold, an event that pays gold are all judged as if the gold were lost, and a shop's own options often tie (run benchmark 15, section 6). The run itself spends its gold once shops are judged in full, so the rollouts play a future the run does not have.

## 2. Runs
- 30 new seeds (`RB016` + index 00000–00029), Ironclad, **Ascension 0**, whole new runs in the simulator (`tools/run_bench.py`). Four policies play every seed.
- `S*`: the stack after run benchmark 15 by that registration's rule (`data/learn/queue/stack2.py final`), fixed by the queue script before any result of this run. `P*`: `S*` with `path` in place of `rpath`.
- Arms: `S*`, `P*`, `S*+bshop`, `P*+bshop`.
- `bshop` (`tools/run_bench.py`): in a shop, buy the cheapest affordable relic until none is affordable, then the card removal once (curses, then Strikes, then Defends), then leave; no card and no potion. It is a rule component: every rollout of the arm is played with it, and in the run itself it acts where the stack's shop rollouts do not decide (exact ties, failures, more options than the component's limit).
- In the `P*` arms the card rollouts (and shop and event rollouts, if in the stack) are unchanged; their futures are played with the route planner in every arm, as before.
- Combat in all arms: `mcw6` in every fight, `--fight-rebuild save+run`. Host: the Release2 build, unchanged. 7 workers claim seeds in index order (`--dynamic`). Stopping rule: unfinished runs stop 7 hours after the start; time the machine is asleep does not count (the rule of run benchmark 12, section 6). The analysis uses the seeds for which all four policies finished.
- Fights and rollout decisions are recorded as in run benchmark 11.

## 3. Policies
- `S*`: map moves by sampled rollouts, ties and failures to the route planner; the shop rule leaves.
- `P*`: map moves by the route planner (`tools/path.py`); the shop rule leaves.
- `S*+bshop`, `P*+bshop`: the same two with the shop rule that buys.

## 4. Metrics and tests
- A factor's effect on a seed: the mean floor of the two arms with it minus the mean floor of the two arms without it (routes: the `rpath` arms minus the `path` arms). H1 and H2: one-sided sign test at 0.05 across seeds, with the mean effect and a bootstrap 95% interval. H3 and H4: per seed, wins with the factor minus wins without (−2 to 2), one-sided exact sign test at 0.05 on the seeds where it is not zero; wins per arm with Wilson intervals. `tools/run_details.py --factor`. The interaction (the route effect with `bshop` minus without) is reported, not tested. The two factors are separate questions; no correction is applied across them.
- Secondary: elites fought per run and their results; node types chosen and shops visited per run; purchases per run by type and gold left; relics, upgrades, potions and HP at each boss fight's start; act bosses beaten; where runs die; how often all options of a decision tie; seconds per run.
- A check that each arm's rollouts were played with its own rules (the lesson of run benchmarks 7, 11 and 12): every recorded decision carries the rollout policy its evaluator used (`rollouts_by`); it must be `bshop+path+pot+smith` in the two `bshop` arms and `path+pot+smith` in the other two, in every decision (`tools/run_details.py --decisions`). Rollouts that failed are reported per arm (`rollout_errors`).
- What follows, the stack that runs next: `rpath` stays if its mean effect in floors is positive and its two arms together do not win fewer runs than the two `path` arms; otherwise `path` replaces it (it is also the cheaper of the two). `bshop` enters by the same rule. A claim either way needs its test.
